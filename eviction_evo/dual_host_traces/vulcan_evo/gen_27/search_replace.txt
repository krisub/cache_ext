<DESCRIPTION>
The current implementation suffers from an overly aggressive cold penalty that harms performance on the dual_small_vs_large workload (throughput dropped from 1601.83 to 1454.21 vs Gen 5). The recommendation suggests adding recency-weighted normalization to distinguish recently-active pages from stale ones.

Key insight: Pages with the same access_count can have very different value — a page accessed 10 times but recently is more valuable than one accessed 10 times but not touched for 10+ seconds.

This change:
1. **Replaces the cold penalty scaling** with a softer, recency-aware approach that proportionally reduces scores for cold pages rather than applying fixed thresholds
2. **Adds exponential recency boost** to base_score to prioritize recently-active pages (decay from +60% within 3s to 0% by 10s), using fixed-point arithmetic
3. **Preserves the heat protection** via interval_ewma but makes it work in concert with recency rather than against it

The rationale: The current cold penalty bucketing (access_count < 5/20) creates artificial cliffs that evict valuable-but-infrequent pages too aggressively. Instead, use a continuous recency function that smoothly rewards recent activity regardless of historical frequency, allowing the network factors and heat tracking to provide necessary protection without over-penalizing cold pages.
