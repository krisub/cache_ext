<DESCRIPTION>
The current program uses a RollingWindow(5) listener for GF_LOST with multi-tiered position-based decay (15% boost for latest, 8% for 1 sample back, 3% for 2 samples back). This approach has a weakness: it decays based on how many newer samples arrive, not wall-clock time, leading to sustained over-protection when traffic is sparse.

The recommendation proposes switching to explicit **timestamp-based decay** for the loss boost. Within the EVOLVE-BLOCK, we track `last_loss_ts` (updated whenever we observe `net_lost > 0`), then apply a linear time-decay formula: `boost = max(100, 115 - elapsed_ms / 10)`. This naturally decays the 15% boost to ~5% after 100ms and becomes negligible after 150ms, regardless of sampling cadence.

This achieves two benefits:
1. **Prevent stale loss signals from locking the cache into overly conservative eviction** — after ~150ms with no new loss events, we revert to baseline protection.
2. **Simpler, more predictable decay** — time-based decay is easier to reason about and tune than position-based history.

Implementation:
- Add a global `last_loss_ts` variable in EVOLVE-BLOCK
- In `bpf_score_fn`, track when `net_lost > 0` and update the timestamp
- Replace the multi-tiered RollingWindow queries with a single time-decay computation
- Simplify GF_LOST config from RW to MINMAX (we use raw `net_lost` directly now)
</DESCRIPTION>
