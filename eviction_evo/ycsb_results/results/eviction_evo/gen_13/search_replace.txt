On high-RTT networks (>50ms smoothed RTT), 5ms inter-arrivals are not actually bursts - they're normal request spacing. This causes false-positive burst detection, which over-expands working set protection (`get_adaptive_working_set_ns()` returns 1s instead of 500ms), leading to cache pollution and poor performance on read-heavy workloads.

1. On low-RTT networks (< 20ms), we still use 5ms as the threshold (preserving current behavior)
2. On medium-RTT networks (20-200ms), the threshold scales proportionally (5-50ms)
3. On high-RTT networks (> 200ms), the threshold caps at a reasonable multiple of RTT

This prevents false-positive burst detection on high-latency connections, tightening protection windows and improving read-heavy workload performance (targeting YCSB-A/B/C improvements).
