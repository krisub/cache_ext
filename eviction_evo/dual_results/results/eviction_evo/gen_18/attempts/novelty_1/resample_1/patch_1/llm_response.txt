<DESCRIPTION>
The current program uses linear recency decay (500 points over the entire recent_window), which may over-protect folios that are slightly older than the window boundary. Looking at the performance metrics, the best prior program (324.29) used tier-specific base windows and achieved better discrimination between recently-accessed and stale folios.

The current program's linear decay allocates points uniformly: a folio at 25ms gets 250 points, one at 50ms gets 0 points (assuming 50ms window). This doesn't sufficiently penalize folios that are "somewhat old" vs "very old".

I propose implementing a piecewise-linear decay curve with steeper initial decay:
- First 40% of recent_window (0-20ms at 50ms base): allocate 350 points (70% of total) with steep decay
- Remaining 60% of recent_window (20-50ms): allocate 150 points (30% of total) with gentler decay

This creates a "knee" in the decay curve that more aggressively evicts folios outside the immediate working set while still providing some protection to moderately-aged folios. The steeper initial slope means folios age out of high-priority status faster, improving cache turnover for the dual_small_vs_large workload where the fast reader needs quick eviction of the slow writer's large values.

This preserves the current program's multi-factor scoring architecture (recency + frequency + burst protection + congestion protection + tier bonus) while improving the recency component's discrimination power.
</DESCRIPTION>
