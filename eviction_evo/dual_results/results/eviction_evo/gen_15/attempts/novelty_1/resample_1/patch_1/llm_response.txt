<DESCRIPTION>
The current program iterates eviction lists in fixed order (cold → warm → hot) regardless of network conditions. During extreme congestion (score > 750), this approach wastes time scanning cold/warm lists when protecting hot folios is most critical for throughput.

The recommendation proposes detecting extreme congestion states and reversing iteration order to hot → warm → cold, using `bpf_cache_ext_list_sample()` with a low sampling ratio (15%) on the hot list to quickly identify and preserve the most critical folios before scanning warm/cold lists.

This optimization reduces eviction latency during congestion by:
1. Prioritizing protection of high-value folios early (hot list first)
2. Using sampling on hot list to avoid full iteration overhead
3. Only doing full scans on warm/cold lists after hot folios are protected
4. Adapting iteration strategy based on real network conditions

The prior programs show consistent improvement when using multi-tier strategies with network awareness. The best performer (324.29 score) used tier-specific windows. This change builds on that by making the eviction iteration itself adaptive to congestion, which should improve p99 latencies in dual_congested (currently 163ms) by enabling faster eviction decisions when network conditions are most variable.
