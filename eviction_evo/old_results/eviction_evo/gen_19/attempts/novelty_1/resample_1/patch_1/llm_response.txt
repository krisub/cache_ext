<DESCRIPTION>
The current program uses a fixed 2ms jitter threshold regardless of RTT, but jitter interpretation is fundamentally RTT-dependent. A 2ms jitter on a 10ms RTT connection represents 20% jitter (severe), while 2ms on a 100ms RTT represents only 2% (negligible). 

The recommendation suggests scaling the jitter baseline by the RTT ratio: threshold = 2ms × (RTT / 100ms), clamped to [0.5x, 2.0x], yielding effective thresholds between 1ms and 4ms.

This adaptive approach will:
1. **Reduce false positives on high-latency connections** (dual_congested with 2-5ms delays will now use 4ms threshold instead of 2ms, making eviction less aggressive inappropriately)
2. **Increase sensitivity on low-latency connections** (clamped to 1ms minimum, catching real jitter earlier)
3. **Better distinguish genuine network stress from normal delay variation** by contextualizing jitter in terms of the connection's baseline RTT

This should improve the dual_congested trace (currently 3109.00 ops/sec) by maintaining more protective eviction during high-latency periods where each cache miss is genuinely more expensive.
</DESCRIPTION>
