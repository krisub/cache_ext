<DESCRIPTION>
The current program regressed from the best prior implementation (2162.27 → 2103.44), particularly on the small_vs_large workload (1645.52 → 1613.83). The recommendation to add size-based score decay aligns with this observation: the dual-client small_vs_large workload has asymmetric value—200B reads are cheaper to evict than 4KB values, yet the current policy treats all folios equally for recency-based protection.

By introducing folio-size awareness to the eviction decision, we can:
1. **Increase eviction pressure on small folios** (< 4KB / 1 page): reduce their protected time window by ~10-15%, making them more evictable. This frees cache space for larger, more valuable entries.
2. **Decrease eviction pressure on large folios** (> 64KB / 16+ pages): extend their protected time window by ~10-15%, keeping large working-set entries in cache longer since they're more expensive to reload.
3. **Maintain stability on the congested workload** by applying size adjustments uniformly across network conditions.

This is orthogonal to the adaptive-window and network-pressure logic already in place, and requires only adding a size lookup and window scaling factor in the eviction callback.
</DESCRIPTION>
