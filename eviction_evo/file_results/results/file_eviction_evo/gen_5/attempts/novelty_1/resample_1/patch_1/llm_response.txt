# Analysis and Proposed Improvement

## Diagnosis

The current dual-list approach has achieved good improvement on `file_priority_throughput` (3285.09), but `file_niceness_throughput` (2510.52) has regressed compared to the third version (2532.74). 

The key issue is **inter-list migration on priority changes**: In the `file_niceness` trace, process priorities FLIP mid-run (process A: nice=-15→15, process B: nice=15→-15 at 60s). When a folio's accessor_nice changes, the folio should migrate between high_prio_list and low_prio_list, but currently:
1. A folio added by a high-priority process stays in high_prio_list
2. When accessed later by a low-priority process, accessor_nice is updated, BUT the folio is never moved to low_prio_list
3. This causes improper over-protection of pages after a process's priority flips down

## Solution

Implement **dynamic inter-list migration** in `folio_accessed` to move folios between lists when their accessor's priority tier changes. This ensures that pages follow their access patterns rather than being locked into their initial classification.

<NAME>
inter_list_migration_on_priority_change
</NAME>

<DESCRIPTION>
When a folio is accessed by a process with a different priority tier than its last accessor, migrate the folio between high_prio_list and low_prio_list. This handles the file_niceness trace correctly where process priorities flip mid-run.

The key insight: in file_niceness, when process A flips from nice=-15 to nice=15, its previously high-priority pages should move to the low-priority list so they can be evicted sooner. Currently they remain in high_prio_list because we only update accessor_nice but don't move the folio.

The fix detects tier changes by comparing is_high_prio_folio(meta->accessor_nice) before and after update. If the tier changes, we:
1. Remove the folio from its current list (bpf_cache_ext_list_del)
2. Add it to the new list (bpf_cache_ext_list_add)

This ensures the page will be evaluated by the correct eviction callback on the next eviction cycle, improving adaptation to priority flips.
</DESCRIPTION>
