<DESCRIPTION>
The current program uses fixed RTT thresholds (5ms and 10ms) for protection scaling, which may be suboptimal across diverse network conditions. The recommendation is to implement adaptive RTT baseline tracking with dynamic threshold scaling.

**Key insight**: Instead of fixed absolute thresholds, track a long-term EWMA of RTT with very low alpha (50) as a workload-specific "baseline," then scale protection dynamically based on deviation from baseline. This approach:

1. **Adapts to workload characteristics**: Each workload (fast direct reader vs. congested dual clients) establishes its own normal RTT baseline
2. **Relative scaling is more robust**: Protection scales based on stress deviation from normal, not absolute values
3. **Smoother transitions**: Uses low-alpha EWMA (alpha=50) for baseline to avoid over-reaction to transient RTT spikes while still tracking long-term trends

The protection factor becomes: `100 + (current_rtt - baseline_rtt) * multiplier`, where the multiplier is tuned to provide 25% extra protection when current RTT is 50% above baseline, and 50% extra protection when it's 100% above baseline.

This replaces the hard-coded threshold checks (>5ms → 1.25x, >10ms → 1.5x) with a principled adaptive model that inherently normalizes for workload-specific network conditions.

The baseline is stored in a single-entry BPF map and updated with fixed-point arithmetic in the kprobe (following the established pattern), ensuring minimal overhead while providing powerful adaptive behavior.
</DESCRIPTION>
