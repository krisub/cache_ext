<DESCRIPTION>
The current program's CWND recovery logic (scaling down bonuses during recovery) has degraded performance from 5667.51 to 5207.70, suggesting we're evicting important pages too aggressively. Instead of scaling down protection universally, we should distinguish between different access patterns and apply asymmetric protection.

LevelDB workloads exhibit two distinct access patterns:
1. **Sequential-hot**: Index scans (YCSB C, F) create very regular, frequent per-folio accesses with low inter-access intervals (<50ms), high access counts, and occur when network is healthy (good CWND). These benefit greatly from aggressive cache protection.
2. **Random-access**: Random key lookups (YCSB A, B, D) create sporadic accesses with high inter-access intervals (>500ms), which are less cache-sensitive.

This change removes the problematic CWND recovery scaling and instead implements per-folio classification:
- Sequential-hot folios: interval_ewma < 50ms AND access_count > 50 AND cwnd_ewma > 20 → +6500 bonus
- Random-access folios: interval_ewma > 500ms → +1500 bonus (reduced from default +5000 hot bonus)

This allows the policy to aggressively protect LevelDB's sequential scan working sets while being more selective about random-access folios, improving throughput on scan-heavy workloads (YCSB C+F, YCSB E+F) while maintaining stability on mixed workloads.
</DESCRIPTION>
