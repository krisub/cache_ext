<DESCRIPTION>
The current program suffers from sustained over-protection when using MINMAX for GF_LOST, as the maximum loss value persists indefinitely even after network conditions recover. This causes the cache to remain overly conservative long after transient congestion events resolve, hurting throughput.

This edit implements two complementary improvements:

1. **Time-decay for loss signal**: Switch GF_LOST from MINMAX to RollingWindow (size 5) to provide natural time-based decay. The rolling window automatically ages out old loss events as new samples arrive, allowing the policy to recover more quickly when network conditions improve. With a window size of 5, loss protection decays over approximately 5 tcp_recvmsg samples, providing ~100-150ms decay time at typical packet rates.

2. **Fine-grained recency tracking**: Replace the coarse 5-second recency bonus with a multi-tier approach that provides:
   - 80% boost for extremely recent accesses (< 100ms): captures active working set
   - Linear decay from 80% to 50% over 100ms-1s: distinguishes hot from warm
   - Linear decay from 50% to 0% over 1-10s: smooth transition to cold

This fine-grained temporal resolution better captures working-set dynamics across multiple time scales, providing stronger protection for the active working set while avoiding over-protection of stale pages. The combination addresses the core issue: loss events trigger appropriate protection during congestion but don't cause sustained over-protection afterward.

The approach mirrors Generation 39's successful recency strategy (2184.76 combined score) while adding the time-decay loss mechanism recommended in the prompt.
</DESCRIPTION>
